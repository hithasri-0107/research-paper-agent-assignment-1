1. CORE CONTEXT EXTRACTION
================================================================================
PROBLEM STATEMENT:
Existing sequence transduction models relied heavily on complex recurrent or convolutional neural networks (RNNs/LSTMs), which process tokens sequentially. This sequential nature prevents parallelization across token positions during training, creating severe compute bottlenecks on long sequences and struggling to retain distant context.

METHODOLOGY:
The authors introduce the Transformer, an architecture eschewing recurrence entirely in favor of stacked Multi-Head Self-Attention mechanisms. Both encoder and decoder blocks map sequence inputs to continuous representations entirely via dot-product attention paired with sinusoidal positional encodings, enabling complete GPU parallelization across all tokens simultaneously.

KEY CONCEPTS:
  1. Multi-Head Self-Attention
  2. Scaled Dot-Product Attention
  3. Sinusoidal Positional Encodings
  4. Transformer Encoder-Decoder Stacks
  5. Residual Connections & Layer Normalization

MATHEMATICAL / ALGORITHMIC FOUNDATIONS:
The foundational equation is Scaled Dot-Product Attention: Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax((Q * K^T) / sqrt(d_k)) * V. Query, Key, and Value vectors are projected into h subspaces (Multi-Head Attention), allowing the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions.

UNIFIED SUMMARY (<300 WORDS):
Traditional sequence models like RNNs process language token-by-token, inhibiting hardware parallelism and causing training bottlenecks on modern GPUs. Vaswani et al. solved this by introducing the Transformer, completely eliminating recurrence and convolutions. Instead, it relies entirely on Multi-Head Self-Attention to compute dependencies between all tokens in parallel. Scaled dot-product attention computes attention weights between projected Query and Key matrices, scaled by the square root of key dimensionality to prevent gradient vanishing in the softmax, then applies these weights across Value vectors. Paired with sinusoidal positional embeddings to preserve token order, residual connections, and layer normalization, the Transformer enables massive batch parallelization, drastically faster training convergence, and superior BLEU scores.
